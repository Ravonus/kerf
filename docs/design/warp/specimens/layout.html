    Layout.

    One element tree, three presentations. A sidebar and header on desktop, a rail on tablet, a bottom bar and top bar on phone, chosen by the container, not the window. Eight primitives do the rest, with spacing that only speaks in relationships. Every nav item, card and the command field carries the sweep.

    01Shells & templates

    Eight pages, one markup, three devices

    Each frame is a real 1280, 834 or 390px viewport scaled to fit, with its own safe areas, pointer and type step. The page inside is identical in all three; only container queries move it. Click around: nav items route (and sweep), ⌘K or the search field opens the palette, the avatar opens the account menu, the rail expands, and on the phone a list row pushes its detail.

      02Primitives

      Eight boxes that only speak in relationships

      No pixel props: gaps are hair · tight · related · group · section, widths are named (rail · list · aside · measure · narrow · content · wide). Drag the corner of any dashed box: each one is a container, so you are testing exactly what a pane, a card or a phone sees.

      03Navigation parts

      Sidebar, rail, bar, crumbs, tabs, docks

      Crops of live shells plus the in-page parts. Hover or tab to a nav item: the sweep draws in left to right and leaves to the right, at every stop. Cards sweep by default at vivid (or with data-flair="sweep"); at quiet and balanced their border simply strengthens.

      03bPage parts & site chrome

      Everything else a page is made of

      Section, PageHeader, Masonry, SplitPane, the draggable FloatPanel, NavList, AnchorNav, TopNav, StickyBar, Banner, AnnouncementBar and the cookie ConsentBanner. Every dashed box is a container: drag its corner past 600 and 1000 to watch the part change form. Nothing here depends on the viewport.

      03cApp chrome & workbench

      Inspectors, tabs, boards and canvases

      The heavier app layouts: a docked inspector, document tabs over docked panes with a status bar, a dashboard board of movable widgets, an infinite canvas, pull to refresh, bleed and inset, back to top and route progress. Each one works by pointer, touch and keyboard, and each one changes form at the same 600 / 1000 container steps.

      04Responsive rules

      What reflows, hides or moves at each step

      Nothing is ever removed from the accessibility tree because the screen is small: it moves (into the bar, a sheet, the palette) or collapses behind one control that names it. Steps are container widths.

      05Navigation motion

      Arrive in place, push in depth, sheets from the edge

      Sibling routes rise a few pixels and fade; hierarchy pushes horizontally; overlays come from the edge they belong to. The dial scales distance and duration; vivid adds the route line (the sweep across the header). Reduced motion keeps every fade and drops every travel. Values below are read live from the tokens.

      06Input & focus

      One focus order on every device

      The DOM order is fixed: skip link, header, nav, main. CSS moves the nav to the left, the edge or the bottom, but a keyboard user who rotates the tablet or widens the window never relearns the order. The one visual mismatch (the phone bar is reached before the page) is covered by the skip link, which is always the first stop.
